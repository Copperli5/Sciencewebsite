What is Physics?

CPhysics is the study of matter, energy, and the fundamental forces that govern how the universe behaves. It explores everything from the motion of everyday objects to the behaviour of light, electricity, and even the structure of the universe itself. This field covers a wide range of topics, including mechanics and motion, waves and sound, electricity and magnetism, and energy transformations. At its core, physics relies on scientific investigation, using experiments, data, and evidence to explain the natural laws that shape how things move and interact. Physics connects closely to everyday life, helping to explain how vehicles move, how electricity powers homes, how sound and light travel, and how forces act on objects all around us. It also underpins many important fields, including engineering, astronomy, technology, and renewable energy, giving us a deeper appreciation of the physical world and the fundamental principles that shape it.

Physics in Level 1

At Level 1, Physics introduces students to fundamental ideas about motion, forces, and energy. Topics typically include speed and acceleration, simple forces, waves, and basic electrical circuits. Students build foundational skills in measuring, calculating, and interpreting physical phenomena through simple experiments and problem-solving tasks. This level provides the basic scientific and mathematical thinking needed for more advanced physics concepts in Level 2.

Physics in level 2

At Level 2, Physics explores more complex topics such as mechanics, wave behaviour, electricity and magnetism, and atomic and nuclear physics. Students are expected to apply mathematical relationships to solve physics problems and explain physical phenomena with greater precision. This level requires stronger analytical thinking and a deeper understanding of the relationships between different physical concepts compared to Level 1.

Physics Level 3

At Level 3, Physics covers advanced topics such as mechanics involving momentum and energy, electromagnetism, wave-particle theory, and modern physics concepts including quantum ideas. Students are expected to critically apply physics principles, interpret complex data, and construct detailed explanations supported by mathematical reasoning. This level reflects the type of scientific and analytical thinking required for tertiary study in fields such as engineering, physics, astronomy, and technology.
